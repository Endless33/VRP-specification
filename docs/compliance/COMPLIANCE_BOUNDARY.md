# Compliance Boundary

**Document Version:** Public v1

**Status:** Public Engineering

---

# Purpose

This document defines the public compliance boundary of the VRP specification.

Its objective is to explain what the public repositories do—and do not—claim regarding standards, certifications, regulatory requirements, and production deployment.

Organizations should perform their own legal, regulatory, operational, and security assessments before adopting any technology.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

Engineering evidence and regulatory compliance are different disciplines.

Successful engineering validation does not automatically imply regulatory approval.

Likewise, regulatory approval alone does not prove engineering correctness.

Both should be evaluated independently.

---

# Public Documentation

The public repositories provide:

- architectural documentation
- engineering methodology
- security documentation
- evaluation guidance
- integration guidance
- engineering evidence
- benchmark methodology
- reproducible validation procedures

These materials support independent technical evaluation.

---

# What VRP Publicly Claims

The public specification describes engineering objectives including:

- Logical Session continuity
- Transport independence
- Replay resistance
- Canonical authority progression
- Deterministic recovery
- Observable engineering evidence
- Fail-closed behavior
- Zero Trust engineering principles

These objectives are documented for engineering review.

---

# What VRP Does NOT Publicly Claim

The public repositories do **not** claim:

- regulatory certification
- compliance with every national regulation
- automatic approval by security authorities
- automatic compatibility with every deployment
- guaranteed suitability for every industry
- universal production readiness for all environments

Organizations should independently determine suitability.

---

# Security Review

Every deployment should undergo an independent security review.

The public documentation is intended to support—not replace—that review.

---

# Compliance Responsibility

Each organization remains responsible for evaluating applicable requirements, including:

- internal security policies
- industry regulations
- legal obligations
- operational procedures
- procurement requirements
- deployment governance

These responsibilities remain with the deploying organization.

---

# Pilot Philosophy

The recommended Pilot exists to reduce engineering uncertainty before production decisions are made.

Pilot participation should not be interpreted as regulatory approval or certification.

---

# Future Evolution

As the public specification evolves, additional engineering guidance may be published regarding deployment practices and security evaluation.

Future documentation should not be interpreted as retroactive certification.

---

# Final Principle

The purpose of the public VRP repositories is to support independent engineering evaluation.

Compliance decisions belong to each organization.

Engineering evidence should inform those decisions.

Regulatory approval should be obtained through the appropriate organizational and legal processes.
