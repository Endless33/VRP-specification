# Who Should NOT Use VRP

**Document Version:** Public v1

**Status:** Public Engineering

---

# Purpose

Not every organization should evaluate VRP.

This document intentionally describes situations where a Pilot is **not** recommended.

The objective is to help engineering teams decide whether VRP is appropriate before investing time in evaluation.

---

# Do NOT Use VRP If...

## You are looking for a VPN replacement

VRP is not designed to replace traditional VPN products.

Its primary objective is verifiable session continuity across changing transports.

---

## You expect instant production deployment

VRP is designed to be evaluated first.

Engineering validation always comes before production decisions.

---

## You are unwilling to verify the technology

VRP is built around reproducible engineering evidence.

If your organization prefers purchasing technology without independent validation, this project is probably not a good fit.

---

## You cannot allocate engineering time

A meaningful Pilot requires engineers who can:

- read documentation
- deploy the evaluation environment
- execute validation
- review engineering evidence

Without engineering participation, the Pilot has little value.

---

## You expect marketing instead of measurement

VRP does not ask organizations to trust presentations.

It asks them to measure.

Run the benchmarks.

Inspect the logs.

Review the evidence.

Then make your own decision.

---

## You are unwilling to challenge the implementation

Healthy engineering depends on skepticism.

Organizations are encouraged to:

- inspect the architecture
- question assumptions
- repeat benchmarks
- perform independent testing

Constructive criticism improves engineering.

---

## You require immediate replacement of existing infrastructure

VRP is designed for incremental evaluation.

Existing infrastructure remains operational during the Pilot.

There is no requirement to replace production networking before validation.

---

## You cannot perform security review

Organizations should complete their own engineering and security review before considering production deployment.

If this process cannot be performed, a Pilot should be postponed.

---

## You expect the protected runtime to become public

The public architecture is intentionally open.

The protected runtime is intentionally not.

This separation is part of the project's intellectual property and security model.

---

# Who SHOULD Evaluate VRP?

Organizations that value:

- engineering evidence
- reproducible validation
- measurable results
- independent verification
- controlled Pilot deployment
- technical transparency

These are the organizations for which VRP was designed.

---

# Final Principle

VRP is not intended to convince everyone.

It is intended to be evaluated by engineers who prefer evidence over assumptions.

If that describes your organization, welcome.

If not, waiting is a perfectly valid engineering decision.
