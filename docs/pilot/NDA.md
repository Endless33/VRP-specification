# Non-Disclosure Agreement (Pilot Summary)

## Status

Public Pilot Documentation

Version: 2.0

---

# Purpose

This document summarizes the confidentiality principles governing participation in a VRP Pilot.

It is not a legal agreement.

Its purpose is to explain the engineering confidentiality model before formal engagement.

Where required, participants may receive a separate legally binding agreement.

---

# Engineering Philosophy

The Pilot is designed to enable independent technical evaluation while protecting proprietary engineering work.

Participants receive sufficient information to evaluate observable runtime behavior.

Protected implementation remains confidential.

---

# Confidential Information

During a Pilot, confidential information may include, but is not limited to:

- protected runtime implementation
- unpublished documentation
- internal engineering notes
- architectural details not included in the public specification
- evaluation procedures
- deployment guidance
- engineering discussions
- commercial information
- future roadmap information

Confidentiality applies regardless of communication medium.

---

# Public Information

The following are generally considered public:

- published documentation
- public repositories
- public engineering articles
- publicly released validation reports
- publicly available presentations

Public information is not subject to Pilot confidentiality.

---

# Participant Responsibilities

Participants are expected to:

- protect confidential information
- limit access to authorized personnel
- avoid unauthorized disclosure
- use confidential information only for the agreed evaluation

Engineering integrity benefits both parties.

---

# Intellectual Property

Participation in the Pilot does not transfer:

- ownership
- copyright
- patent rights
- implementation rights
- commercial rights
- licensing rights

All protected technology remains the property of its respective owner unless explicitly agreed otherwise.

---

# Reverse Engineering

Participants agree not to intentionally use Pilot activities for:

- implementation reconstruction
- source code recovery
- protocol cloning
- extraction of proprietary mechanisms
- unauthorized derivative development

The Pilot exists to evaluate engineering behavior rather than reproduce implementation.

---

# Engineering Discussions

Technical discussions are encouraged.

However, discussions do not imply permission to disclose protected implementation.

Questions regarding observable runtime behavior are welcome.

Questions seeking confidential implementation details may remain unanswered.

---

# Evidence

Engineering evidence generated during the Pilot may be reviewed according to the applicable Pilot agreement.

Evidence supports engineering evaluation.

Evidence does not grant implementation access.

---

# Responsible Disclosure

If participants discover:

- unexpected runtime behavior
- implementation concerns
- security issues
- documentation inconsistencies

they are encouraged to report them privately.

Responsible engineering communication benefits all participants.

---

# Duration

Confidentiality obligations continue beyond completion of the Pilot unless otherwise specified in a formal agreement.

---

# Applicable Agreements

This document summarizes engineering expectations.

Formal Pilot engagements may include additional legally binding documents covering:

- confidentiality
- intellectual property
- commercial terms
- liability
- permitted use

Those agreements take precedence where applicable.

# Summary

The VRP Pilot balances openness and protection.

Observable runtime behavior is available for independent engineering evaluation.

Protected implementation remains confidential.

This separation enables meaningful technical validation while preserving long-term intellectual property.

> Protect implementation.
